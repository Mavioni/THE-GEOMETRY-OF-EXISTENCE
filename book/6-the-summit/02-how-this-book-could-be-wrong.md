# How This Book Could Be Wrong

A book that claims to defeat every system is lying, because the systems contradict one another; anything that satisfied all of them would contradict itself. This book aims at something harder and more honest. It answers every rival’s best argument in that rival’s own terms, changes where the argument wins, and declares openly where it chooses a different side. It states how strong each claim is, and how each could be shown wrong. Then it asks you to test it. That is the only kind of proof a book about living can have. The evidence in this edition was reviewed as of November 2026; where the science moves, the book will move with it.

Aristotle warned that we should seek as much precision in each subject as its nature allows, and that ethics is true “roughly and in outline.” The book’s commitments are defended as philosophers defend principles: by testing them against our considered judgments until the two agree. Its practices are defended as scientists defend claims: by evidence, and by predictions that could fail. Its analogies are defended only as pictures.

## The Rivals’ Ledger

Every major system’s best argument against this book, and the answer.

### The Skeptic *Pyrrho and his heirs*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** No claim can be known with certainty.

**OUR ANSWER.** Agreed, which is why this book claims no certainty, only stated strength and open tests. The skeptic lives by appearances and custom; this book offers better customs and invites the skeptic to test them.

### The Error Theorist and the Nihilist *Mackie; Nietzsche*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** No moral claim is objectively true, and kindness is the morality of the weak.

**OUR ANSWER.** The book’s practical claims have the form “if you want trust, do this,” which even an error theorist accepts. Its commitments are chosen, not claimed as facts written into the universe. And its power thesis is no resentment of strength: it bets that given power is the stronger kind. Where we still differ: we hold that the floor is worth choosing.

### The Egoist *Thrasymachus, Callicles, Stirner, Rand*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Justice is the advantage of the stronger; look after yourself.

**OUR ANSWER.** Answered on the egoist’s own terms: over fourteen years, selfish and aggressive people gained no more power than generous ones, because their damaged relationships cancelled their gains. A long-sighted egoist should choose earned power. Conceded: in a perfectly hidden, one-time act, self-interest alone cannot forbid wrong. There, integrity is defended as part of who you are.

### The Machiavellian *Machiavelli; Greene’s 48 Laws*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** It is safer to be feared than loved.

**OUR ANSWER.** Machiavelli also warned the prince never to be hated, which is the very mechanism the evidence shows. Greene’s laws win the move; these laws win the relationship. Know which game you are in, and in fear-ridden games, protect yourself.

### The Utilitarian *Bentham, Mill, Singer*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Do whatever produces the most good overall, even if a few must pay.

**OUR ANSWER.** Accepted: impartial care, giving from surplus, and concern for every being that can suffer. Declared side: the floor of dignity is not traded for aggregate gain. Beware the greater good that requires a lesser person.

### The Kantian *Kant*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Worth cannot be earned, and the Golden Rule lacks content.

**OUR ANSWER.** Agreed on both counts, and repaired: dignity is unearned, only appraisal respect is earned, and the Golden Rule is stated in its reversible form, as what you would accept standing in the other’s place, with their values.

### The Virtue Ethicist *Aristotle, MacIntyre*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** A cross-traditional manual floats free of any living tradition.

**OUR ANSWER.** This book is a tradition in the making, with its own practices, ledger and errata: “an argument extended through time,” which is what MacIntyre says a living tradition is.

### The Contractualist *Rawls, Scanlon*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Talents and starting places are morally arbitrary; a ledger of conduct alone is unfair.

**OUR ANSWER.** Accepted: the Starting Balance. The Ledger records what you did with what you were given, never a verdict on what you were given.

### The Care Ethicist *Gilligan, Noddings, Kittay*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Ledgers of achievement erase care, and invulnerability is a false ideal.

**OUR ANSWER.** Accepted: care counts as conduct in the Ledger, and others may inform your temperature even though they may not set it. Kittay’s insight, that we are all some mother’s child, now grounds the floor itself.

### The Existentialist *Sartre, Camus*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Existence precedes essence; laws smuggle in an essence.

**OUR ANSWER.** The First Principle agrees: nothing outside you grants your worth. The laws are maxims chosen and chosen again, not laws of nature.

### The Buddhist *The Buddha; Nagarjuna*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** There is no fixed self to respect, and clinging to a manual is clinging.

**OUR ANSWER.** On the conventional level, where ethics lives, persons are real; on the ultimate level, held lightly. The parable of the raft fits this book exactly: it is for crossing the river, not for carrying on your back once you are across.

### The Daoist *Laozi, Zhuangzi*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** When the Way is lost, rules appear; codes are a symptom.

**OUR ANSWER.** Conceded in part: the laws are training wheels, meant to be outgrown into spontaneous rightness. Much of this book is already Daoist: the light hold on outcomes, the peace that needs no guard, the power the people barely notice.

### The Confucian *Confucius, Mencius*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Respect follows roles: parents and offices are owed it regardless.

**OUR ANSWER.** Accepted: role courtesy is a third tier, offered to offices and bonds and revocable by abuse, as Mencius taught when he said a tyrant forfeits the throne.

### The Christian *Paul; James*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Worth and salvation are grace, not earnings.

**OUR ANSWER.** This book is in its structure a book of grace with an ethics of conduct on top: the floor is given, never earned; only respect among people is earned, which James and the Gospels affirm when they judge trees by their fruit. It makes no claim about salvation.

### The Muslim *The Qur’an 17:70*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** God has honoured the children of Adam; dignity is His gift.

**OUR ANSWER.** Agreed: the floor is a gift. A believer may hold every commitment here as an expression of faith; a non-believer may hold them without it. That is overlapping consensus in practice.

### The Hindu *The Gita; the teaching of karma*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Action bears fruit; each has a dharma.

**OUR ANSWER.** The Ledger is close kin to karma, and the Gita’s last instruction ends this book. One caution kept: karma has been misused to justify caste, which is why the Starting Balance clause exists.

### Ubuntu and Indigenous Ethics *Nguni teaching; Anishinaabe and Haudenosaunee traditions*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Personhood is conferred and sustained by community.

**OUR ANSWER.** This is the deepest support for the floor as now grounded: the infant and the elder are persons because they are held. Kept apart: humanity (the floor) from personhood as excellence (the ceiling).

### The Marxist and Critical Theorist *Marx; the Frankfurt School*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** “Earn your reality” blames the poor for what structures did.

**OUR ANSWER.** Accepted as the most dangerous misreading of the title, and answered in the text: work on your own ledger alone, and on unjust structures together.

### The Game Theorist *Axelrod; Nowak*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Defection pays in one-shot games.

**OUR ANSWER.** Accepted and printed: Section V of the Geometry shows the exact boundary. Reciprocal cooperation is safe against every rival once the chance of meeting again reaches two-thirds; below that line, protect yourself.

### Disability and Animal Ethics *Kittay, Nussbaum; Singer, Regan*

**THEIR BEST ARGUMENT.** Grounding dignity in ability excludes some humans and arbitrarily excludes animals.

**OUR ANSWER.** The first objection won, and the book changed: dignity belongs to every someone held in relation, whatever their abilities. On the second, a declared line: every being that can suffer is owed consideration; every human being is owed the full floor.

## The declared sides

- The floor of dignity is not traded for aggregate welfare. (Against strict utilitarianism.)
- These values are chosen and defended, not claimed to be written into the universe. (Against error theory, without pretending to refute it.)
- The ethics of this book can be held with God or without, as an expression of faith or apart from one. (Compatible with grace; not with a strict divine-command theory.)
- Full dignity for every human being; real moral consideration for every being that can suffer. (A line drawn openly where Singer and Regan would draw it differently.)
- Integrity is kept even where no one could ever find out. (Against the Ring of Gyges.)

## Twelve predictions

1. In long-lasting groups, people who gain rank by intimidation will, a decade later, hold no more power than those who gain it by respect, and will be less liked.
2. Under acute fear and uncertainty, support for dominant leaders will rise.
3. Delaying and reappraising will reduce aggression compared with venting.
4. Forgiving first-time or agreeable offenders reduces repeat harm; forgiving disagreeable repeat offenders without change increases it.
5. Restorative conferences will satisfy victims more than standard processing, with equal or lower reoffending.
6. People who loosen materialist values will show small gains in well-being.
7. There is no single income above which money stops mattering for everyone.
8. People who improve their close relationships will tend to show better health.
9. Most readers will not find a new practice automatic by day thirty; it will take two to three months.
10. Teams that keep authentic dissenters will decide better than teams with appointed devil’s advocates.
11. Nonviolent campaigns will prove at least as effective as violent ones, at far lower human cost. This is the most contested of the twelve.
12. A reader who runs the Ninety-Day Trial with an outside rater will improve on at least one chosen behaviour beyond their starting trend. If most careful readers do not, this book is wrong about its practices.

## The errata promise

Each edition of this book will publish what changed and why. A claim that survives scrutiny earns its place. A claim that fails is removed, and the removal is recorded, never silently erased. This edition records four: the economy of base three is a little over five percent, not six; the leading ritual study is described as disowned by its own authors; the re-test of the 1974 memory study is described as being organized, not under way; and the claim about nonviolent campaigns now carries the 2026 challenge to it.

The book earns its reality the same way it asks you to earn yours.
