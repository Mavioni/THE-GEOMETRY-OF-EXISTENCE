# Sources and Translations

### Translations used

Quotations from ancient texts come from public-domain translations: the King James Version of the Bible; George Long’s Epictetus and Marcus Aurelius; James Legge’s *Analects*, *Mencius* and *Tao Te Ching*; F. Max Müller’s *Dhammapada*; E. B. Pusey’s Augustine; T. K. Abbott’s Kant. Kipling’s “The Law of the Jungle” (1895) and “If —“(1910) are in the public domain. Modern authors under copyright are paraphrased or quoted only briefly, with attribution. The verses that close each chapter are new, written for this book in the manner of Kipling’s ballads; they quote no one.

### Mathematics and the plates

- L. Zadeh (1965) on fuzzy sets; H.-J. Zimmermann and P. Zysno (1980) on how people combine criteria.
- The E8 root system and its Coxeter-plane projection; the Kamea of the Sun; rose curves. All figures computed for this edition, and every sum checked by machine.
- The chapter plates are computed from the laws they name: the geocentric path of Venus from two circular orbits (periods 1 and 0.6152 years); the analemma from the standard approximations for the equation of time and solar declination; potential flow past a cylinder; the nodal lines of a square-plate Chladni mode; a damped two-pendulum harmonograph; a catenary; a ruled hyperboloid; and seeded branching rules for the neuron, the root and the kintsugi fracture.

### History and living traditions

Historical examples were checked against standard references. The George III remark about Washington comes secondhand through Benjamin West. Napoleon’s words to Talleyrand vary between memoirs. Kennedy’s absences from the Missile Crisis meetings are reported in Robert Kennedy’s *Thirteen Days*. Ashoka’s casualty figures are his own royal claim. The origin of PLUR is credited to Frankie Bones but poorly documented. The Anishinaabe Seven Grandfather Teachings and the Haudenosaunee Great Law of Peace belong to their communities and are described here with respect, not as their authoritative voice.
