# The Ninety-Day Trial

This is how you put the book to the test. It is built like a small experiment, because your own life is the only laboratory that finally counts. Run it honestly and the result will be yours, whichever way it falls.

|  |  |
|---|---|
| DAYS −14 TO 0 | Take a baseline before you change anything. Each day, rate your well-being from 0 to 10, log every conflict and how it went, and count three behaviours you chose, such as promises kept, angry replies sent, or minutes at home base. |
| DAY 0 | Write your prediction: what you expect to change, and by how much. Seal it in an envelope, or give it to a friend to keep. |
| DAYS 1–30 | Practise: the morning question, the pause before anger, the floor for one difficult person each day, three small promises, one minute at home base three times a day, the nightly review. |
| DAYS 31–37 | Pause the daily practices, but keep measuring. If things slip, that is evidence the practices were doing something. |
| DAYS 38–90 | Practise again, and add the weekly Ledger and one act of repair. |
| E VERY WEEK | Ask one person who sees you often to rate, from 0 to 10, how calm, kind and reliable you were. An outside eye corrects the hopeful eye. |
| DAY 90 | Open the envelope. Compare yourself with your own starting trend, not just your starting point, because hard seasons often mend on their own. Write down what worked, what did not, and what you will keep. |
