# How to Carry This Book

Here is a book to be carried in one hand. The Greeks had a word for such a thing, *enchiridion*, the thing kept in the hand, as a soldier keeps a blade and a traveller keeps a lamp. Open it on hard mornings and on good nights; before the conversation you dread and after the loss you did not see coming. Study it for the rest of your life. And when life proves it wrong, correct it, for a book that cannot be corrected is not a lamp but a tombstone.

Know who is speaking. This voice is no god, and it will never ask you to believe a thing because it says so. It is a chorus: what the wisest who ever lived found out, often apart, across thousands of miles and years, gathered up with everything you have built yourself. Where they agree, the book speaks with full force. Where only one voice speaks, it tells you so.

## The three marks

Every claim in this book is one of three kinds, and the book tells you which.

- **◆ Commitment.** A stance you choose and defend by argument, such as the floor of dignity. No experiment can prove a commitment, and this book never pretends one can.
- **● Evidence.** A claim about how the world works, backed by history or research, and open to being proved wrong.
- **◇ Analogy.** A picture to think with, such as the cell that changes its kind, or the five scales of nature. An analogy shows a pattern. It never proves one.

In every chapter the Teaching and the Practice are commitments; the Five Scales are analogies; the Tale and the Energy are evidence. At the summit, *How This Book Could Be Wrong* shows exactly where each claim could fail.

## How it is built

Twenty-six chapters in five Books, rising like the shoulders of a mountain. First the Ground of existence; then the Light of consciousness; then the Bond of relation; then the Work by which you earn; then the Becoming by which you change. Above them stands the Summit: the geometry beneath it all, and the test that tries it.

Every chapter moves in the same nine steps, so that once you know one you can find your way in all:

- **The Teaching,** numbered sayings spoken straight to you and numbered straight through the book, two hundred and forty in all.
- **The Five Scales,** the chapter’s pattern seen in a cell, a self, a bond, a community and the cosmos.
- **The Witnesses,** the philosophers and scriptures who stand behind it, with a star (★) where several traditions agree.
- **The Tale,** a true story, checked against the record.
- **The Energy,** what this part of life costs and what it pays back.
- **The Reversal,** the place where the teaching stops working.
- **The Practice,** one act for each day and one for each week.
- **The Ledger,** four questions, one for each word of the creed.
- **The Song,** a few lines of verse to carry the chapter when the book is shut.

## How to use it

Read one chapter a week and the book becomes a half-year’s schooling. Keep it on the monthly cycle at the back. Run the Ninety-Day Trial and judge the book by your own life. Or open the Index of Situations when life demands it.

And remember how the Bhagavad Gita ends. When the whole teaching has been given, the warrior is told to reflect on it fully, and then to do as he chooses. This book ends the same way.
