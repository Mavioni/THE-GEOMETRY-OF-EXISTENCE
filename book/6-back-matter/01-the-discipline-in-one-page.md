## The Discipline in One Page

<p align="center"><i>Everything in the five levels that can be carried on a card.</i></p>

**The search.** Five steps, adapted from *ISOMORPHIC: The Field Manual*.

1. **Name the binary.** Write the two options as one sentence. A binary you can see is no longer doing its job.

2. **Find the premise underneath.** It is usually a sentence nobody in the argument has said aloud in years.

3. **Attack the premise, not the options.** Arguing between the options is how you stay inside the frame.

4. **Look one layer down.** If your third can be described in the vocabulary of the original two, it is a compromise.

5. **Own that layer.** A third on rented ground is a discovery made on someone else’s behalf.

Steps one to four are cheap and feel like progress. Step five is expensive and feels like none.

**The compression test.** Three questions, in order, in writing. *What constraint made this compression rational? Does the constraint still hold? If not, what is holding it now — and who benefits?*

**The degree.** Draw the line. Anchor both ends in words a stranger could check. Mark it. Name what would move the mark up and what would move it down. Where several things are required, take the lowest. Then act whole, with a date.

**The falsifier.** One sentence, written before the hard week: *I would know I was wrong about this if —* It must name something that can be observed.

**The day.** Morning: what today is Given, what must be negotiated, what could be built. Through the day: one touch of the middle when you catch the capture. Evening: where did the attention actually go.

**The marks.** *Lived* — tested only by doing. *Argued* — reasons given, no one compelled. *Demonstrated* — follows from stated definitions; recomputed. *Measured* — observed, with its source. *On record* — a matter of published history. Let no kind of knowing wear another’s clothes.
